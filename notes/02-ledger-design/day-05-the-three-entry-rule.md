# Day 05 — The Three-Entry Rule (Cash, Cost, Permission)

Most confusion in restricted nonprofit accounting comes from mixing three different events:

1. **Cash received** (bank changes)
2. **Expense incurred** (cost changes)
3. **Revenue earned** (permission changes)

These are related — but they are not the same.

---

## Scenario A — Cash received with conditions
**Plain English:** “We received money with rules, so we’re holding an obligation.”

Typical entry:
- **Dr Bank (1060)**
- **Cr Unearned / Deferred Grant Revenue (2150)**

This happens when grant money comes in (deposit, EFT, cheque).

---

## Scenario B — Expense incurred
**Plain English:** “We spent money or incurred a cost.”

Typical entry:
- **Dr Expense (e.g., program supplies, wages)**
- **Cr Bank (1060)** or **Cr AP / Accrued Liabilities**

This happens whenever the organization purchases, pays, or accrues a cost.

---

## Scenario C — Revenue earned (eligibility)
**Plain English:** “Because we followed the rules, part of that restricted money is now earned income.”

Typical entry:
- **Dr Unearned / Deferred Grant Revenue (2150)**
- **Cr Restricted Grant Revenue (e.g., 4250)**

This happens when eligible costs occur, not when cash arrives.

---

## Why this matters
If you separate these three events, you get:

- cleaner fund reporting
- faster month-end close
- grant balances that actually mean something
- audit-ready statements
- fewer “mystery variances” between programs and the GL

---

## Operational approach (fast + auditable)
- AutoEntry / bank feeds handle **Scenario A** and **Scenario B**
- **Scenario C** is posted monthly as a summarized journal entry
  based on eligible program expenses

The result is architecture-level clarity: truth becomes routine, not a quarterly project.
